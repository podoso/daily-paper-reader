# LineageRAG: Harnessing GraphRAG by Constructing Evidence Lineages with Source Grounding

Abstract 

Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GraphRAG) retrieves evidence for multi-hop questions over structured cor-pus graphs. Existing GraphRAG methods leave the connection between evidence discovery and source grounding implicit. We propose LineageRAG, which constructs one evidence lin-eage for each query-derived evidence demand and completes it with a verbatim source span when the selected evidence supports that demand. LineageRAG first initializes the evi-dence demands. It then expands each lineage through demand-conditioned retrieval over the corpus graph while retaining the demand associated with every candidate. Lineage completion uses this provenance to select complementary passages and grounds supported demands in verbatim source text. Experi-ments on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue show that LineageRAG improves R@5, EM, and F1 by 3.51, 5.96, and 5.22 points on average over leading GraphRAG baselines. 

# 1 Introduction 

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) supplies a language model with evidence retrieved from an external corpus (Lewis et al. 2020). Conventional retrievers score passages independently, which works when one passage contains the required evidence. Multi-hop questions often depend on bridge passages whose relevance emerges through previously discovered evidence. Graph-based RAG (GraphRAG) ad-dresses this dependency by organizing corpus units through structured relations. HippoRAG propagates relevance across an OpenIE graph using Personalized PageRank (Gutiér-rez et al. 2024). HippoRAG 2 deepens passage integration through online language-model use (Gutiérrez et al. 2025). PropRAG retrieves proposition paths (Wang and Han 2025). HGRAG uses a cross-granularity hypergraph (Wang et al. 2026). Graph-based retrieval finds relevant candidates, but the final evidence set does not record the requirement behind each source. This missing connection can leave GraphRAG with an incomplete evidence chain. Figure 1 presents a rep-resentative GraphRAG failure on HotpotQA. Previous work uses query structure at different points in retrieval. Question decomposition preserves subqueries dur-ing candidate discovery (Ammann, Golde, and Akbik 2025). SETR uses explicit information requirements during evi-dence selection (Lee et al. 2025b), while RichRAG retains aspect–document associations during ranking (Wang et al. 2025a). Relink organizes retrieved evidence into a query-specific graph (Huang et al. 2026). NeocorRAG organizes retrieval around evidence chains (Peng et al. 2026). These methods improve coverage during discovery, ranking, or se-lection, but do not carry the same demand–passage associa-tion through graph retrieval to verbatim source evidence. Lineage tracing offers a natural model for this missing history. In biology, it identifies the descendants of a marked cell (Kretzschmar and Watt 2012). Data lineage applies the same principle to derived records by identifying the source items that produced them (Cui, Widom, and Wiener 2000). For GraphRAG, we define an evidence lineage as the trace that carries one evidence demand from graph discovery to its verbatim source evidence. We propose LineageRAG, a GraphRAG framework that constructs and maintains these lineages in three stages. 

Evidence-Demand Induction derives query-specific de-mands, each specifying what the retrieved evidence must es-tablish. Demand-Conditioned Lineage Expansion retrieves candidates for each demand and preserves their demand as-sociations during candidate merging. Lineage Completion with Source Grounding uses this provenance to select com-plementary passages and extract source-anchored verbatim spans from the selected passages. Retaining such a span com-pletes the corresponding lineage. Our contributions are as follows. 

• We formulate evidence lineage as a trace that follows each Evidence Demand from graph discovery to verbatim source evidence. It keeps the provenance of each demand explicit while the final passage set is constructed. 

• We introduce LineageRAG, which preserves each Ev-idence Demand’s identity from candidate discovery to grounded source evidence. # 2 Related Work 

GraphRAG. GraphRAG represents corpus knowledge or query-specific evidence as a graph and retrieves through its relations. HippoRAG propagates relevance over an OpenIE graph (Gutiérrez et al. 2024), and HippoRAG 2 strengthens the integration of passages and graph retrieval (Gutiérrez et al. 2025). PropRAG searches proposition paths with an online beam search (Wang and Han 2025). LightRAG com-bines graph and vector retrieval at two granularities (Guo et al. 2025), while HGRAG retrieves over a cross-granularity hypergraph (Wang et al. 2026). G-reasoner trains a graph foundation model over its QuadGraph abstraction (Luo et al. 2026). SUBQRAG stores triples from sub-question reason-ing as graph memory (Li et al. 2025). AGRAG presents a reasoning subgraph with the retrieved chunks (Wang et al. 2025b). These systems improve evidence discovery through graph structure. LineageRAG also keeps the connection be-tween each Evidence Demand and its retrieved sources avail-able during passage selection and source grounding. 

Query decomposition and evidence selection. Question decomposition preserves subqueries during candidate dis-covery and merges their results for reranking (Ammann, Golde, and Akbik 2025). DeCoR uses decomposed queries and compressed evidence to initiate a second retrieval stage (Yun and Kim 2025). SETR uses explicit information re-quirements during evidence selection (Lee et al. 2025b), while RichRAG retains aspect–document associations dur-ing ranking (Wang et al. 2025a). Relink constructs a query-driven evidence graph (Huang et al. 2026), NeocorRAG or-ganizes retrieval around evidence chains (Peng et al. 2026), and ETS searches over candidate evidence sets with a tree (Sun et al. 2025). LineageRAG preserves each Evidence De-mand’s identity through retrieval, selection, and grounding to a verbatim source span. 

# 3 Problem Formulation 

Let q be a query and D = {di}Ni=1 a passage corpus. For d ∈D, text( d) denotes its source text. G is the graph index over this corpus. The parameter k specifies the context size. An 

Evidence Demand is a query-specific evidence requirement. The method produces 

Oq = ( Sk, W q ), Sk = ( d(1) , . . . , d (k)) ∈ D k, (1) where Sk is the ordered list of selected passages and Wq is the set of grounded annotations. The passages in Sk are distinct. Each annotation w = ( iw, d w, s w, a w) links demand iw to span sw in selected passage dw. The term aw records the resulting typed relation. 

# 4 LineageRAG 

LineageRAG carries each Evidence Demand from graph dis-covery to a grounded source span in three stages. Figure 2 illustrates these stages with an example. Figure 2: Overview of LineageRAG. Evidence-Demand Induction identifies facts required by the query. Demand-Conditioned Lineage Expansion retrieves candidates for each demand and preserves demand–passage provenance. Lineage Completion with Source Grounding selects complementary passages and grounds each supported demand in a verbatim source span. 

Evidence-Demand Induction 

An inference-time language model maps q to an ordered sequence of Evidence Demands using a fixed prompt PD :

Rq = Normalize(LLM θ (PD , q )) = ( r1, . . . , r m). (2) 

rj = (id j , δ j , I j , O j ), 1 ≤ j ≤ m ≤ 5. (3) The description δj states the fact that evidence must establish. 

Ij records the symbolic variables consumed by the demand, and Oj records those its evidence should establish. They are not values resolved from retrieved evidence. All demands are induced jointly from q. In Figure 1, r0 identifies the shuttle and r1 requests its launch year. The identifier id j remains fixed through retrieval, selection, and grounding. The induction prompt asks the model to express the re-quired facts as ordered demands: 

Evidence-Demand Induction Prompt PD

Decompose the query into ordered Evidence Demands whose joint resolution makes the answer derivable. Each demand states one fact that a source passage must estab-lish. Separate evidence identifying an intermediate entity from evidence establishing the answer. The complete prompt and JSON schema are provided in the supplementary material. 

Demand-Conditioned Lineage Expansion 

For each Evidence Demand, LineageRAG retrieves a ranked passage list from the same graph index: 

Lj = Retrieve G (q, δ j ) = ( dj, 1, . . . , d j,h ), (4) where h is the retrieval depth. The retrieval query formed from q and δj determines the graph seeds. Personalized PageRank propagates the seed scores over G and ranks pas-sages to form Lj . Each call uses (q, δ j ) independently and does not substitute values resolved for earlier demands. The order of Rq controls depth-wise list merging and de-mand presentation to selection and grounding. A passage en-ters Cq = ( c1, . . . , c M ) at its first occurrence, with M ≥ k.Let τ (d) be its normalized title. The retained provenance and selector representation are 

Aq (d) = {(id j , ℓ ) | d = dj,ℓ }. (5) 

bq (d) = ( Aq (d), n q (d), ρ q (d), g q (d)) , d ∈ Cq . (6) 

Aq (d) preserves the demand ID and rank of every retrieval event that exposed d. The count nq (d) = |A q (d)| summa-rizes repeated exposure. The statistic ρq (d) is the best rank recorded in Aq (d). Same-title redundancy is represented by 

gq (d) = |{ c ∈ Cq : τ (c) = τ (d)}| .During merging, LineageRAG retains each candidate’s de-mand IDs and within-demand ranks in Aq (d). The selector can then distinguish candidates retrieved for different Evi-dence Demands and construct a passage set that covers com-plementary requirements. 

Lineage Completion with Source Grounding 

Set-wise selection. The selector evaluates the merged can-didate pool against Rq . Each candidate excerpt is augmented by bq (d):

Sk = Select k(q, R q , C q , {bq (d)}d∈Cq ). (7) The selector evaluates candidates jointly to build the passage set. The provenance in bq (d) reveals whether a candidate contributes to an uncovered demand. It can therefore favor a complementary passage even when a redundant passage is ranked higher in the merged pool. The selector applies this provenance through the following instruction: 

Set-Wise Selection Prompt 

Use each candidate’s demand-specific retrieval provenance to select k passages that jointly cover the Evidence De-mands. Favor complementary facts and avoid evidence that repeats a covered demand. Returned IDs are validated against Cq and deduplicated. The retained passages follow candidate-pool order, and the earliest unselected candidates fill any remaining positions up to k. The complete selector protocol appears in the supple-mentary material.

cWq = Extract( q, R q , S k). (8) The joint grounding call uses Ij and Oj to populate the 

consumes and produces bindings. Evidence lineage preserves demand identity across stages without sequential query rewriting. The extraction prompt is: 

Source Grounding Prompt 

For each supported Evidence Demand, return its source passage and the verbatim span that establishes it. Return no record for unsupported demands. The proposed annotations are filtered by 

Wq = {w ∈ cWq : Valid q (w)}. (9) Validity first anchors the proposal to a selected source: 

dw ∈ Sk and sw ⊑norm text( dw). Here ⊑norm is sub-string containment after whitespace and case normalization. The schema check VΓ(aw) = 1 verifies the typed annotation. # 5 Experiments 

We evaluate LineageRAG through four research questions: 

• RQ1. How does LineageRAG compare with leading GraphRAG methods on multi-hop QA benchmarks? 

• RQ2. How do the components of LineageRAG contribute to retrieval and answer quality? 

• RQ3. How do evidence-set construction and source grounding account for LineageRAG’s retrieval and QA gains? 

• RQ4. What is the computational cost of LineageRAG? 

Experimental Setup 

Datasets. We evaluate on three multi-hop benchmarks: HotpotQA (Yang et al. 2018), 2WikiMultiHopQA (Ho et al. 2020), and MuSiQue (Trivedi et al. 2022). Following Hip-poRAG 2 (Gutiérrez et al. 2025), we use 1,000 queries from each dataset and its corresponding retrieval corpus. 

Baselines. The baselines are grouped as follows: (1) LLM-based retrieval: NV-Embed-v2 (Lee et al. 2025a). (2) Con-ventional RAG: BM25 (Robertson and Zaragoza 2009) and RAPTOR (Sarthi et al. 2024). (3) GraphRAG: Hip-poRAG 2 (Gutiérrez et al. 2025), PropRAG (Wang and Han 2025), HGRAG (Wang et al. 2026), and G-reasoner (Luo et al. 2026). (4) Evidence selection methods: ETS-7B (Sun et al. 2025), Relink (Huang et al. 2026), and NeocorRAG 

(Peng et al. 2026). 

Metrics. Retrieval is evaluated with supporting-passage R@5, and QA with exact match (EM) and token-level F1. Source Grounding is evaluated by demand coverage and GPT-4o demand–span support judgments, with GPT-4o-mini judging the same samples independently. The full audit pro-tocol is provided in the supplementary material. 

Implementation details. Qwen3-32B (Qwen Team 2025) constructs an OpenIE graph with passage and entity nodes connected by extracted relations, passage–entity edges, and entity-similarity edges. At query time, it runs the three Lin-eageRAG stages. Retrieve G uses HippoRAG 2 PPR with a damping factor of 0.5, and the matched baseline uses the same graph index and retrieval settings. NV-Embed-v2 (Lee et al. 2025a) supplies dense embeddings, and GPT-4o-mini (OpenAI 2024) serves as the QA reader. All generative calls use temperature 0. Runnable baselines use the best settings reported in their papers. All rerun methods use the same corpus, query split, and GPT-4o-mini reader. ETS-7B and Relink use the published results obtained with their native readers. Full prompts, retrieval settings, candidate budgets, and baseline configurations are provided in the supplemen-tary material.

Does Source Grounding improve answer generation? 

Table 7 fixes the selected passages and their order, chang-ing only whether grounded spans are provided. Adding them raises EM from 36.50 to 42.70 and F1 from 50.11 to 55.76. Because retrieval is identical, the spans introduce no new sources and leave the passage set and order unchanged, iso-lating their effect on reader-side evidence use. In the passage-only condition, the reader must locate support within the pas-sages and infer which demand each passage resolves before combining facts. Grounded spans make both decisions ex-plicit through demand-indexed evidence cues, allowing the reader to compose facts whose locations and roles are already identified. The gains in both EM and F1 indicate better exact-answer recovery and broader answer-token coverage. Source Grounding therefore improves how the reader uses selected evidence while leaving evidence availability unchanged. Computational Cost (RQ4) 

Table 8 separates one-time graph construction from query-time model use. LineageRAG and HippoRAG 2 share the same graph index, whose construction consumes 13.49M to-kens. HippoRAG 2 follows one whole-query retrieval path. LineageRAG additionally induces demands, retrieves for each demand, selects from a provenance-augmented pool, and extracts grounded spans for the reader. These stages preserve demand identity from candidate discovery through source grounding and increase query-time token consump-tion from 0.63M to 1.51M tokens per 1,000 queries, or 880 additional tokens per query. The total for one graph con-struction and 1,000 queries rises from 14.12M to 15.00M. Because graph construction is shared, the total difference comes from these query-time lineage operations. This 2.40-fold increase in query-time token consumption accompanies gains of 3.53 R@5, 6.82 EM, and 5.73 F1 over HippoRAG 2. The ablations attribute 2.15 R@5 points to Lineage Expansion and 6.20 EM and 5.65 F1 points to Source Grounding. These gains locate the return from the additional computation in both evidence construction and answer generation. PropRAG uses 18.84M tokens offline and none online, while NeocorRAG uses 6.94M online. 

# 6 Conclusion 

In this work, we propose LineageRAG for multi-hop GraphRAG to address the missing connection between ev-idence discovery and source grounding. LineageRAG in-duces Evidence Demands and expands each through graph retrieval while preserving demand–passage provenance. It then uses this provenance to construct a complementary ev-idence set and ground supported demands in source text. Experiments on three multi-hop QA benchmarks show im-proved retrieval and answer quality over leading GraphRAG baselines and demonstrate the value of evidence lineage for source-grounded multi-hop reasoning.
